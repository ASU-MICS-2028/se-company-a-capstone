# P01: patient, health centre (23 Nov 2026)

**Who:** an adult woman who runs her own business, with an employee. She was visiting with two children. She uses a smartphone.
**Where:** a community health centre in Greater Accra, on a quiet morning.
**How:** in person, mostly in Twi, about 9 minutes. Recorded with her agreement. The audio was transcribed by an AI tool and then deleted. Interviewed by Joseph Etse and a second team member.
**Removed from these notes:** her name, places that could identify her, and the health details she volunteered.

## What she said

- **Wait:** at clinics in general, it's "sometimes more than two hours, sometimes one hour, it depends."
- **Cost:** if you work for someone, you ask permission to leave. If you run your own business, you either close it or leave a worker in charge. She has a worker but still has to step away herself.
- **Travel:** she lives only a few minutes away by car, but "sometimes you stand by the roadside for about 1 hour" before a car comes. **Travel time depends on whether transport is available, not on distance.** For emergencies she goes to a referral hospital further away, which also needs a car.
- **Short-code booking:** she said she would use it, "but sometimes emergency…". Emergencies have to bypass booking.
- **What she really wants is to know before she goes.** Is the doctor in? Is the service she needs available? Can she come now? "Sometimes when you go, they will tell you, 'Oh, doctor is not around'… you pass through all the procedures, then when you get there…" She compared this to the MoMo USSD menu, where you know where to go for each thing. She also wants to skip "unnecessary procedures" for a routine check-up.
- She told a second-hand story about an emergency that was turned away because no doctor was on duty, and it ended badly. It shows how strongly people feel about "doctor not around".

## Notes on the interview itself

- The short-code idea was pitched around the fifth question, before we had heard her full last-visit story. The "would you use it" answer is therefore weak evidence (hypothetical and leading). Next time, pitch only in part D.
- No exact times were captured for her last visit. Use guide part B ("Tell me about the last time…") next time.
