# Interview guide (#29)

**Status: draft for Liza to edit.** About 15–20 minutes, in person or by phone, in whatever language the person prefers. Each member does 1–2 interviews.

**Who to talk to:** someone who visited a public OPD recently: market traders, shop or office workers, older relatives, parents or carers who accompany patients. One clinic staff interview (nurse, records clerk, or the Ashesi clinic) is worth several patient ones. For staff, use part C.

## Rules

- Get consent first (script below). No names in notes: use P01, P02… (patients) and S01… (staff).
- Ask about **waiting**, never about illness. If someone volunteers health details, don't write them down.
- Ask about **what happened**, not what they think they'd do. "Tell me about the last time…" beats "Would you…?"
- Don't pitch Twɛn until part D. Before that, you're learning, not selling.
- Audio only with explicit consent, deleted after the notes are written.

## Consent script

> I'm a student at Ashesi University. My team is designing a way to cut waiting time at hospital outpatient departments. Can I ask you about your last visit? It takes about 15 minutes. I won't write down your name or anything about your health, and you can stop at any time. Is that OK?

## A. Warm-up (2 min)

1. What do you do day to day? *(work pattern, flexibility)*
2. What phone do you use? Do you use SMS, USSD (like `*170#`), WhatsApp?

## B. The last visit (8 min): get the story in order

3. Tell me about the last time you went to the OPD. When did you decide to go?
4. What time did you leave home? Why that time?
5. What happened when you arrived? *(folder, records, vitals, queue)*
6. How long did you wait at each step? What did you do while waiting?
7. Did you ever leave the queue and come back? What happened?
8. What did that day cost you? *(income, school, transport, childcare)*
9. What was the worst part? What was the least bad?
10. Has anyone ever told you how long you'd wait? Was it right?

## C. Staff version (replace B)

3. Walk me through a normal morning. When do patients start arriving?
4. How do you decide who goes next? Who can jump the queue, and who decides?
5. What happens when someone isn't there when called?
6. What tools do you use: paper, folders, a computer? What happens when the network or power goes?
7. What would make the morning easier for you? What would you never accept from a new system?

## D. The idea (4 min), only at the end

> Imagine you could join the queue from your phone, get a ticket number and an estimate of your wait, then get one text telling you when to come.

11. What's your first reaction?
12. What would make you trust that text enough to arrive later?
13. What would worry you?
14. Which language should the messages be in?
