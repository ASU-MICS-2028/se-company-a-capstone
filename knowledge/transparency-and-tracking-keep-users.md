# Transparency and tracking keep users

Walking through the drone food-ordering app as if he were using it, the lecturer made two UX points:

**Let people look before they commit.** He dislikes apps that force login before you can see anything. It is more inclusive to let people browse and even add items to a basket, then say "it's easier if you register, but you can also just fill this form". Forcing registration up front loses customers.

**Make progress visible.** He described a pizza ordering system abroad with animations and timers: your place in the queue, the pizza going into the oven, a countdown to ready, then pickup, then delivery. If something goes wrong (say the rider has an accident), the rider taps a reason on their phone, GPS confirms the delay, and the system recalculates and tells the customer. Don't promise 30 minutes and deliver in 45 without explanation.

His rule: **transparency is very important**. An informed customer is happy; if the system is a black box, they go to a competitor.

## Use it in the capstone

Design our prototype to show users the status of whatever the system is doing for them (progress, delays, reasons), and avoid unnecessary sign-up walls. Cite this in the UX doc.
