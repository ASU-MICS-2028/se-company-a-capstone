# Partial inclusion and stating limits

Asked whether products built without some users in mind are non-inclusive, the lecturer said:

- If you do not provide a service for a group, it is good practice to **document it** somewhere, for example a footnote.
- Be careful with wording: in print it can read as discrimination. Not saying anything may be understood as "we lacked the money to do everything".
- If you cannot deliver a fully inclusive package, **provide some interventions**. If you can't build them, use a plugin; if you can build them, you avoid monthly fees and can offer them free to all. Meet people halfway.
- **State assumptions.** Example: "we assume hearing-impaired users have vibration enabled on their phones" is a reasonable assumption, but it must be written down. (Phones usually have vibration on by default, and user settings override the app.)

Even if users have their own assistive setup (screen readers, translation apps), build your system as if they have no support. Businesses don't want customers to feel unwelcome.

## Use it in the capstone

Add an "assumptions and exclusions" list to the requirements doc, alongside constraints, and mention it in the paper's limitations.
