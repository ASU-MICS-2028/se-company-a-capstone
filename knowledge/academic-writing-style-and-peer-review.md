# Academic writing style and peer review

**Writing style.** A research paper is not a lab report or a project report. "There's a science to writing" it. The lecturer's rules:

- **Never use "I".** Write impersonally (for example, "the system was evaluated…", or "this paper presents…").
- Write a precise, descriptive document, and justify claims with evidence and citations.
- Read recently published papers similar to yours to learn the style.

The course is also quietly teaching **research methods**: justify every idea and back it with literature.

**Peer review.** Reviewers interrogate every element of a paper: the metrics, the regression results, everything. Some reviews are harsh. The lecturer's advice:

- Read every comment, and respond to **each point one by one** in a rebuttal.
- Some criticism comes from reviewers who misunderstood you. Some points are real gaps, and those can seed your next paper.
- Journals can take years to publish. Revise, resubmit and keep going.

Other researchers may later cite your work and point out its gaps. Take that seriously too.

## Use it in the capstone

Do an internal "reviewer 1 / reviewer 2" pass on our draft before submission. Treat the lecturer's feedback at the presentation as a review that needs a point-by-point response in the revised paper.
