# Harte et al. 2017: three-phase HCD methodology

**Argument.** Human-centred design (HCD) keeps users involved throughout development, but its slow, iterative nature clashes with fast product cycles. The authors propose a structured, scalable HCD method that keeps user needs central without losing speed.

**Context.** WIISEL, a connected-health system of wireless insoles and a phone app that estimates fall risk in older adults from gait and balance. Users are elderly, unsupervised at home and often not tech-savvy, so usability is a safety issue.

**Method: three phases,** mapped to ISO 9241-210:
1. **Context of use and user requirements.** Build a *use case document* with user profiles, scenarios, storyboards, life-size screenshots and paper prototypes. Walk users, clinicians and experts through it, collect Likert-scale and interview feedback, cluster the problems by heuristic, and give each a severity rating.
2. **Expert inspections.** [Heuristic evaluation](heuristic-evaluation.md) and [cognitive walkthroughs](cognitive-walkthrough.md) by small multidisciplinary groups, on prototypes built from Phase 1 feedback.
3. **Usability testing with end users.** Lab, then field, using think-aloud and metrics such as the System Usability Scale (SUS).

After each phase, problems are logged and fixed, and the process can loop back.

**Findings.** The method was applied successfully to WIISEL and uncovered usability and human-factors problems before deployment. The authors argue it scales to other connected-health products and to projects of different sizes.

**Concepts to reuse:** [HCD per ISO 9241-210](human-centred-design-iso-9241-210.md), [usability definition](usability-iso-definition.md), [severity rating](usability-problem-severity-rating.md), [universal design](universal-design.md).

## Use it in the capstone

Cite this in the paper's Methodology as the basis for our UX process. A cut-down version fits 6 weeks: use case document, then a team heuristic review, then 3–5 user tests with SUS.
