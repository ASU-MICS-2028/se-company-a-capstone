# Empiricism in Scrum

**Empiricism** means making decisions from evidence and experience, not from feelings or habit. The lecturer called it the base of Scrum. Scrum's three pillars all rest on it: **transparency** (everyone can see the work and the facts), **inspection** (checking progress often) and **adaptation** (changing the plan when the facts say so).

He applied it in two directions:

1. **Research facts.** Before committing to a feature, look up the current best way to build it, quickly. Any decision should be backed by research the whole team can see.
2. **Team experience.** People who have built something similar can estimate it better and move faster, so give them that kind of task.

**No emotions, just facts.** If a senior member says "I've done it this way for fifteen years" and a new member shows a faster modern tool, the team picks whichever the evidence favours. The senior member accepts it. To settle a disagreement: give each side about five minutes to present its option, then decide as a team.

Emotions still exist. They get handled in the [sprint retrospective](sprint-retrospective.md), not by winning arguments in planning.

## Use it in the capstone

Back architecture and tooling decisions with short written evidence (a paper, a benchmark, or past experience). It makes design rationale easy to defend in the C4 docs, the ACM paper and the interview.

## Also in Lecture 5

- When forming a team, try to include at least one person who has done this kind of project before; their experience shapes the trajectory. Pure theory needs guidance.
- Sprint planning requires rigorous research: what has been done before, which technologies were used, what we can use. "Let's do something with AI" is not enough - say why. If you can't justify it, it doesn't pass planning, and the team must approve before an increment starts.
- Transparency means decisions are visible and agreed by all. Seek consensus, but if one member is persistently unreasonable, the team may proceed.
- Truthfulness is essential: "I'm almost done" when you are not damages the timeline for everyone.

## Also in Scrum Guide 2020 (in personal notes)

The Scrum Guide adds **lean thinking** (cut waste, focus on essentials) alongside empiricism, and links the pillars in a chain: *transparency enables inspection* (inspecting hidden work misleads) and *inspection enables adaptation* (inspecting without changing anything is pointless). Adapting is hard when people aren't empowered or self-managing. The five Scrum values (commitment, focus, openness, respect, courage) are what make the pillars work.
