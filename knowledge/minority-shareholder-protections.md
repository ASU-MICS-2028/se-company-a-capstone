# Minority shareholder protections

**Minority shareholders** own too little of a company to control its decisions. In a **private company** (one whose shares are not traded on a stock exchange) they are especially exposed, because they cannot simply sell their shares and leave.

Protections one group proposed in the Lecture 6 seminar:

1. **One share, one vote.** Every shareholder gets a say in proportion to what they own. No super-voting classes.
2. **A clear exit route.** An agreed way for minority holders to sell when things go wrong, so they aren't trapped with the fallout.
3. **An independent board and independent chair**, plus separation between the parts of the organisation.
4. **Information rights.** Regular financial reports, audits, and access to investigation findings, so nothing important is hidden from them.

These all serve the same aim: people with less power can still see what is happening and act on it.

## Use it in the capstone

The software version is **information rights for stakeholders**. Give our client or stakeholders and the lecturer read access to the repo, the backlog and the sprint records, so progress is visible without having to ask.
