# Product backlog vs sprint backlog

The **product backlog** is the long, ordered list of everything the product might need. Each item is usually a user story. It grows from early talks with the client about what they want at a high level. It may be vague and incomplete, and that is fine. Don't spend much time estimating it. Order it by client priority and research instead (see [MoSCoW prioritisation](moscow-prioritisation.md)).

The **sprint backlog** is the subset the team pulls from the product backlog and commits to during [sprint planning](sprint-planning-three-questions.md) for one sprint (one fixed work cycle, often one or two weeks). The stories are broken into small, actionable tasks.

Worked example from class (an accessible cafeteria menu). The product backlog might hold 25 stories. For sprint 1 the team takes three:

1. Browse daily menu by category: design menu, add images, grey out dishes that are not available, group by category.
2. Add items to cart and review order: add-to-cart button, change quantity, remove item, running total, place-order button.
3. Accessible order confirmation: check colour contrast, announce the confirmation through the screen reader.

The lecturer's advice:

- **Don't be overly ambitious.** If planning shows that only five of eight wanted items fit, say so. Push the other three to the next sprint.
- **Be transparent with the client.** If you promise five, deliver five.
- **The team takes the items as a commitment.** It should finish them by the end of the sprint.

## Use it in the capstone

Our Scrum records should show one product backlog plus a separate sprint backlog for each sprint, with tasks under each story. Leftover work that moves to the next sprint should be visible.
