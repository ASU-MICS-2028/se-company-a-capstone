# Capstone presentation format

In-class, conference style, at ASERC on **18 Dec 2026** (practice run 17 Dec):

| Part | Time |
|---|---|
| Technical talk | 15 minutes (may grow to 20, in line with a conference slot) |
| Live demo, on the deployed system | 5 minutes |
| Moderated Q&A (the lecturer will try to bring in another instructor) | 10 minutes |

That is about 30 minutes. With six people per company, each speaker gets roughly 2 to 3 minutes. The brief says presentations "must reflect professional industry and academic standards". Slides are a required upload.

Weight: the brief gives presentation and demo **20%** (see [grade weights](capstone-grade-weights.md)). Rubric section E (15%): **Clarity and organisation 7%** (exemplary: engaging, professional) and **Visuals and delivery 8%**.

Earlier course presentations show the order the instructor likes: problem, user, requirements, prototype demo, evaluation, defence (Prosit 1's 10-minute format). Defence means saying why the client should adopt the design.

Q&A and the individual interviews test whether each member can defend decisions. The rubrics reward explaining *why*, with evidence, over listing.

## Use it in the capstone

Draft a 15-minute talk that can stretch to 20, built around the rubric: problem and African relevance, users, architecture and patterns, intelligent component, quality numbers, ethics, results. Rehearse the demo on the live URL with a recorded fallback. Every member should be able to answer questions on their own area, and on architecture and reliability.
