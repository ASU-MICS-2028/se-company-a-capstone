# Human-centred design (ISO 9241-210)

**User-centred design (UCD)**, a term from Don Norman in the 1980s, puts the end user at the centre of design. ISO 9241-210 widened it to consider all stakeholders, not only direct users, and called it **human-centred design (HCD)**: an approach that makes interactive systems more usable by focusing on how the system is used and by applying human-factors and usability knowledge.

**Four activities** (repeated in cycles):
1. Understand the users and specify the context of use.
2. Specify user requirements.
3. Produce design solutions.
4. Evaluate the designs against the requirements.

**Six requirements** a process must meet to count as HCD:
1. The design rests on an explicit understanding of users, tasks and environments.
2. Users take part throughout design and development.
3. User-centred evaluation drives and refines the design.
4. The process is iterative.
5. The design addresses the whole user experience.
6. The team has multidisciplinary skills and perspectives.

ISO's stated benefits: productivity, user well-being, less stress, better accessibility, lower risk of harm.

## Use it in the capstone

Use the six requirements as a self-check in the paper's Methodology section. Show evidence for each one, for example user tests in sprint 2 and sprint 4 (requirement 4, iterative).
