# Usability, human factors and UX defined

Three related terms that are often mixed up:

- **Usability** (ISO): how far specified users can reach specified goals with **effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction** in a specified context of use.
- **Human factors** (ANSI/AAMI): applying knowledge of human abilities and limits (physical, sensory, emotional, intellectual) to the design of tools, systems and environments.
- **User experience (UX):** a person's perceptions and responses that come from using a product or service.

Usability testing is often scored on **five components**: learnability (easy the first time), efficiency (fast once learned), memorability (easy to return to), errors (few, and recoverable), and satisfaction (pleasant to use).

Usability is not cosmetic. Harte et al. note that good products fail because of poor interfaces, while mediocre ones succeed with friendly ones.

## Use it in the capstone

Write usability non-functional requirements with measurable targets. For example: "a first-time user completes an order in under 2 minutes with no help", or "SUS ≥ 68". Test them in sprint review.
