# Board independence matters most in good times

An **independent director** has no personal or financial ties that make them loyal to the CEO, so they can question the CEO freely.

In the Uber case, the founder handpicked most senior executives and board members. They were meant to be independent, but they were loyal to him, and their investments depended on his success. So they did not question him early. The session facilitator's lesson: **board independence matters most when things are going well**, because that is when nobody wants to rock the boat and problems quietly grow.

Points raised in the group presentations:

- A board filled with investors tied to the CEO has a **conflict of interest**. Members acted only once their own money was at risk.
- The board publicly backed the CEO while some directors privately wanted change. Groups judged that this was not responsible governance.
- Directors pushed for months to hire a COO and CFO with no result. On paper the board had power, but in practice it could not use it.
- **What stronger oversight would look like:** independent directors and an independent chair, plus committees, such as one for people and culture complaints, so grievances reach the board.

## Use it in the capstone

Get reviews from someone outside the work: a peer team, the lecturer, or a real stakeholder at the sprint review. Don't only have teammates approve each other's work. Record outside feedback in the Scrum records.
