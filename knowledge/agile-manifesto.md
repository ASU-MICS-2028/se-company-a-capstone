# The Agile Manifesto

Agile methods were introduced to fix plan-driven rigidity: requirements can **evolve** from a small idea, there is **continuous collaboration**, **frequent incremental value** (each worked subset of requirements becomes a deliverable increment, and increments add up to the product), and **rapid feedback**.

The **Agile Manifesto** states four values as "X over Y". The lecturer stressed how to read them: the items on the **right are still done**; the items on the **left are the priority**.

1. **Individuals and interactions over processes and tools** - talk within the team and with stakeholders to understand the purpose before procuring tools. Tools are used, just not the focus.
2. **Working software over comprehensive documentation** - documentation exists, but working software comes first. The stakeholders, in constant collaboration, decide whether it works.
3. **Customer collaboration over contract negotiation** - engage the customer directly to learn what they want (see [rolling contracts](agile-contracts-use-milestones.md)).
4. **Responding to change over following a plan** - there is a plan, but when change is needed, fix it and continue.

**Extreme Programming (XP)** is an agile method where developers may even sit in the stakeholder's office, so questions get answered at once and the client already understands the product when it is delivered. Other agile methods include **Scrum**, **Kanban** and **Crystal**; each has a different structure.

## Use it in the capstone

Quote the four values in the paper when justifying Scrum, and show the "left side" in our records (stakeholder sessions, working increments) while still keeping the docs the rubric needs.

## Also in Personal notes: Agile Methodologies/Terms/Agile Manifesto (Agile Manifesto, 2001)

The twelve principles behind the values, condensed: satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery; welcome changing requirements, even late; deliver working software often (weeks rather than months); business people and developers work together daily; build around motivated people and trust them; prefer face-to-face conversation; working software is the main measure of progress; keep a sustainable pace; pay continuous attention to technical excellence and good design; **simplicity: maximise the work not done**; the best designs come from self-organising teams; reflect and adjust at regular intervals.

**Use it in the capstone:** "maximise the work not done" is our scope rule for a 6-week project. Cut features, not quality.
