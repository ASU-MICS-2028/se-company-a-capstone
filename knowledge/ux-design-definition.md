# What user experience design is

The lecturer's definition: **user experience (UX) design** is the process of analysing **all potential users** of a system, considering all their needs - including needs the users themselves have not noticed - and using that to create an **enjoyable** experience for them.

Three words carry the weight:
- **Potential**, not just obvious, users. Students and faculty use a room, but so do guest lecturers, cleaners, technicians, visitors and firefighters.
- **Unobserved needs** - things users would not list but still need.
- **Enjoyment** - move past "it works" (functionality) to "I like using it".

He closed the lecture by saying you should never build a system without considering user experience; getting it right makes the product far more effective.

## Use it in the capstone

Our UX doc should show the full list of potential users, their needs (stated and unstated), and how the design aims for enjoyment, not just function.

## Also in Personal notes: User Design Experience/Terms/User Experience Design

From my UX notes (Beare and Allanwood): traditional design runs Conceive → Design → Build → Test. The user appears only at Test, and the team goes back to the drawing board only if the product fails. UX design is holistic and brings the user in from the start. Because it is an approach rather than a fixed procedure, someone should own it. Large teams have a UX director or strategist. In small teams it is added to a front-end or software engineer's role.

Values UX should embody: knowledge, understanding, respect, empathy, purpose and productivity. Norman's point (paraphrased): working and usable is not enough; products should also bring pleasure.

Related ideas in the notes: *evolutionary design* ("breeding faster horses", improving what exists) vs reimagining the need; *expert review* (specialists decide for users); *task modelling* (break a goal into the tasks a user must complete).

**Use it in the capstone:** name a UX owner in our Scrum role table, even though everyone builds.
