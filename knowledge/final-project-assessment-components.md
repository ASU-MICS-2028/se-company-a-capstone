# Final project assessment components

What the lecturer said about the final project:

**Timeline**
- The cafeteria system is the running example up to mid-semester. After that, teams have about seven to eight weeks for the final project.
- From Lecture 9 to a week after the mid-semester break, teams build their solutions while applying each topic as it is taught: UX, then diagrams, then architecture.

**What is assessed (three parts)**
1. **The project** itself, built using **Scrum**.
2. **An interview** (see [mid-semester interview and exam](mid-semester-interview-and-exam.md)).
3. **A research paper at IEEE/ACM conference or journal level.** It can focus on an AI element (models, dataset cleaning), cybersecurity, or software engineering, or combine them. He wants teams to publish: "it's not going to be a pet project." He mentioned invitations from journals, including one on digital health.

**Minimum deliverables**
- **At least four or five types of diagram** (use case, sequence, activity, flowchart and others) **plus the architecture**.
- At worst, if a tangible working system can't be built, at least a website or mobile app that conveys the idea, plus **recommendations for implementation**.
- A **budget**, in case the idea gets implemented.
- Work will be shown to real stakeholders (for the cafeteria: the campus facilities team, cafeteria management, and possibly the provost). "It's very, very serious."

**Professionalism:** choose a professional company name, report it to the lecturer, and run the team like a proper working company.

## Use it in the capstone

Check this list against our plan: Scrum records, at least five diagram types plus C4, a budget, a deployed prototype, a publishable ACM paper, and a stakeholder showing. Our company name is Nyansapo Systems.
