# UX research methods

Fifteen common methods, by stage (from the UX textbook chart in my notes):

- **Propose / Discover:** field study, stakeholder interview, task analysis, focus group, competitor analysis.
- **Create:** persona creation, scenario development, journey map, card sort, prototype.
- **Deliver / evaluate:** usability test, heuristic evaluation, expert review, A/B test, eye tracking.

Ways to research the user's world: interviews (one to one), focus groups (five or more), field study (observe users where they are), generative methods (users produce diaries or video logs), and listening to real or representative users.

What each method reveals (the grader's distinction):
- **Stated behaviour:** what people *say* (interviews, surveys).
- **Observed behaviour:** what they *do* (observation, usability tests).
- **Contextual experience:** how setting shapes use (contextual inquiry, diary studies).

For each method you pick, explain its purpose and why it fits this context. Don't just list it.

## Use it in the capstone

Pick two or three methods we can actually run in week 1–2 (for example, 5 short interviews plus one observation session). Report the method, sample and key findings in the proposal's stakeholder section and the paper's Methodology.
