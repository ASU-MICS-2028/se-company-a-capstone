# Capstone grade weights (and where the documents disagree)

The project is worth **100 points = 40% of the final course grade** (brief, page 1).

**Brief, section 6 (summary weights)**
| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Project proposal (Milestone 1) | 10% |
| Software prototype (MVP) | 40% |
| Documentation package | 10% |
| ACM research paper | 20% |
| Final presentation and demo | 20% |

The lecture recording sounded like Presentation was 10%, but the written brief says **20%**, so go with the brief.

**Detailed rubric image (the "detailed rubric provided separately")**
| Section | Weight |
|---|---|
| A. Software prototype | 25% |
| B. Documentation package (includes the ACM paper at 5%) | 20% |
| C. Scrum project management record | 10% |
| D. Technical architecture and design | 15% |
| E. Presentation and communication | 15% |
| F. Ethical, social and impact considerations | 10% |

The tables do not match: the rubric has no proposal line, gives the paper only 5%, and scores Scrum, architecture and ethics separately.

**Syllabus.** "Final Exam (Team Project, Research – 30%, Individual Interviews – 10%): 40%". Its schedule table instead lists Research Paper 10%, Software Product 15%, Individual Interview 15%. So an individual interview probably sits on top of the team project.

**Individual contribution.** The lecturer has used individual scores in team projects before and may again. He plans to sit in on some team meetings to see whether everyone contributes, whether the chair lets everyone speak, and whether good HR practices are followed. Each member should become the expert in their assigned area.

## Use it in the capstone

Treat the detailed rubric as the marking sheet, but do not neglect the proposal or the paper. Raise the mismatch once, in writing, and record the answer here. The working, deployed system is the biggest share of either table. Keep visible records of who did what (Scrum board, commits, meeting notes).
