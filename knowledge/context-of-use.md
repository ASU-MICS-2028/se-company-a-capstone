# Context of use

The **context of use** is the situation in which someone uses a design: *who* they are, *why* they use it, and *where and when* (place, time pressure, noise, connectivity, device). UX design is a user-centred approach that judges a product by how people interact with it *in that situation*, not in a lab.

Examples: Akornor at lunchtime is crowded and noisy, with users short on time. Harte et al.'s elderly users wear insoles at home, unsupervised, while doing chores.

**Creative storytelling** when the budget is small, from my UX notes:
1. Paint the scenario (the current situation).
2. Tell the story (what happens, what could go wrong, what could go right).
3. Identify features (the system's requirements).

Specifying the context of use is the first activity in ISO human-centred design.

## Use it in the capstone

Write one context-of-use paragraph per persona in the proposal (place, device, connectivity, time pressure). Use it to justify non-functional requirements such as offline mode or low data use.
