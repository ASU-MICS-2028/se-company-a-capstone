# Legitimate reason versus legitimate process

The groups debated whether the investors' demand that Uber's CEO resign was legitimate governance or a power play. The investors delivered it in person, outside a formal board meeting, weeks after his mother died, with a deadline of about a day.

The conclusion several groups reached:

- **The reason was legitimate.** He was causing real harm to the company and its people.
- **The process was not.** There was no framework to follow, so the investors had to use force. A *legitimate reason* is different from *legitimate governance*.
- **Ethical versus fair.** Approaching him while he was grieving was unethical towards him. But it could be called fair to the company and its staff, who were being harmed. Groups described it as a balance with arguments on both sides.
- **Motive.** The investors acted only after the problems became public and threatened their returns. In protecting their money they also protected the company.

**What a transparent removal process looks like:**

- decided in a formal board meeting, by an independent board
- clear grounds and a documented procedure, agreed in advance
- checks and balances, with no single person holding all the votes
- time for the person to respond

## Use it in the capstone

Agree in advance, and in writing, how the team handles a member who isn't contributing: warning, Scrum Master conversation, then escalation to the lecturer. A fair process then exists before it is needed.
