# Privacy versus security

The two are linked but not the same, and the lecturer expects you to know the difference.

- **Privacy** is your right and ability to control how your personal information is collected, used and shared. It means keeping valuable assets out of sight of people outside your domain.
- **Security** is the technical measures (locks, firewalls, encryption, access control) that stop unauthorised people getting into a system or its data.

**House analogy.** Someone owns a house and an expensive handbag. A passer-by sees the bag through a huge glass window, and temptation and a plan follow. Lowering the window, moving it or removing it is a *privacy* step: common sense, so nobody can see the asset. Adding an electric fence, barbed wire or reinforced doors is a *security* step: standard technical defences that stop people getting in.

Privacy can be a by-product of security. Online, you should know who has access to your data. Anyone who gets it without permission has breached your privacy.

A lesson from a large crypto exchange hack: the public-facing security was strong, but the back door was not. Secure the direct routes to the database too, not only SSH and the front end.

## Use it in the capstone

The proposal's ethics section and the requirements should list privacy NFRs (what personal data we collect and who sees it) separately from security NFRs (authentication, encryption, access control).
