# Specific prompting with pattern keywords

Recent industry interviews at Ashesi (a large financial-data firm, with others reported to do the same) gave candidates a system design task, such as a food ordering app, and *let them use AI*. Candidates who had memorised data structures and Big O were caught off guard. The interviewers read every prompt and judged:

- **Prompt quality.** "Build a very effective food ordering system" is something any high-school student could type. What marks a software engineer is precise terms in the prompt: "use the Observer pattern", "priority queue", "idempotency keys".
- **Debugging approach.** When you got an error, how did you prompt your way to a fix?

The feedback was that candidates were good, but their AI prompting could have been better.

**Why engineers stay relevant.** The lecturer's view: AI output is generic (many AI-built websites look the same). A car's diagnostic reader finds faults, but the mechanic fixes them. In the same way, a human must architect the system, adapt it to specific needs, fix problems and stay *in the loop* for decisions. He calls himself a software architect rather than a developer, because AI now covers much of the development work.

## Use it in the capstone

Our prompts for building the prototype should name the patterns, data structures, algorithms and NFRs from our design docs. Keep the important prompts in the repo as evidence of process.
