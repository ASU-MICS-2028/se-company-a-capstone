# Prosit 1: inclusive Akornor cafeteria UX

**Problem.** Akornor cafeteria is crowded, noisy and short on time at peak hours. A digital ordering system built for the "average student" may exclude some users. Two teams designed the same journey for different users: Team 1 for visually impaired students, Team 2 for students with ADHD.

**Central question.** How might we design an ordering experience that lets students with different accessibility needs order *independently, efficiently and confidently*?

**Scope.** One critical journey, in about 6–8 Figma screens: Home → Menu → Meal details → Cart → Checkout/Payment → Confirmation → Order status. Starting requirements FR1–FR9 run from browse to track. Non-functional requirements cover usability, accessibility, performance, security, privacy, reliability, responsiveness and error tolerance.

**One-week plan.** Day 1: problem, stakeholders, facts vs assumptions vs questions, persona, journey. Day 2: at least 12 requirements, classified and prioritised as Must, Should or Could. Day 3: information architecture, user flow, low-fidelity wireframes. Day 4: clickable high-fidelity prototype. Day 5: a timed test scenario, at least 3 problems with severity and fixes, then a 10-minute talk.

**Marks (100):** problem and persona 10; journey 10; requirements 20; traceability 15; Figma prototype 20; accessibility 15; evaluation 5; defence 5.

Key ideas: avoid stereotypes; trace every screen back to a requirement; compare the two teams to find shared needs and one **universal design** opportunity.

## Use it in the capstone

The same package (persona, journey, classified requirements, flow, wireframes, rapid evaluation) is what documentation section B2 expects. Reuse the method, at a larger scale.
