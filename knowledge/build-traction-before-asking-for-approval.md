# Build traction before asking for approval

After the campus ride-service shutdown, the lecturer expected any new transport-related idea to face resistance from the institution. His strategy for the bamboo bike:

1. **Start small and visible.** Put a bike or two around campus, add components, and let people ride and ask "what is this?"
2. **Let interest grow** before going to the decision-makers. If you ask on day one, the answer will be "please don't."
3. **Then pitch formally** to the facilities team and the provost, framing it as a **private-sector collaboration with students**: the outside company consults the student team, and the lecturer is the technical lead. Make the case "foolproof", with safety policies in place (see [safety incidents can end a product](safety-incidents-can-end-a-product.md)).

He also described the institution as careful about new things but quick once a step is grounded. Earlier drone projects had people asking "what's happening with the drone?" a year later, which shows interest that lasts.

The general idea: evidence of real use is the best argument to a cautious stakeholder.

## Use it in the capstone

Plan a small pilot with real users before the 18 Dec showcase, and report usage numbers or feedback in the paper's evaluation and the conference talk.
