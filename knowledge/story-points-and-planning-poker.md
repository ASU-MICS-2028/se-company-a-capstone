# Story points and planning poker

**Story points** measure effort in relative units, not hours. Teams usually use the Fibonacci numbers (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13…) because the growing gaps reflect how much less certain big items are.

**Planning poker** is a way to agree on points. Each team member privately picks a card with a number for an item, everyone reveals at once, and the people with the highest and lowest numbers explain why. Then the team re-votes until it agrees.

What the class worked out with the lecturer:

- **Score the tasks, then add them up for the story.** Give points to each small sprint-backlog task. A story's total is the sum of its tasks. Smaller pieces are easier to estimate than one big story.
- **Story totals are for comparing stories.** Use them to decide which story to pull into the sprint first. Task points tell you how to run the work inside a story.
- **Don't point the whole product backlog.** It is too vague early on. Order it by priority instead.
- **A faster variant.** Full poker, where everyone votes on every item, is slow. In industry the developer who owns the task often bids a number. A tech lead who knows that person's capacity accepts it, pushes back, or adds help.
- **Some teams also put a cost on each item.**

The lecturer said to study other estimation methods beyond Fibonacci, including more rigorous ones, and lighter prioritisation schemes such as [MoSCoW](moscow-prioritisation.md). He hinted this could come up in the interview.

## Use it in the capstone

Show points on every sprint-backlog task in our Scrum records, and note which method we used. The planned-versus-done points per sprint also make a simple velocity chart for the paper or the talk.
