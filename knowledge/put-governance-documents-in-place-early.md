# Put governance documents in place early

The session facilitator (a lawyer by training) told entrepreneurs: your main worry is making money, so governance feels like someone else's problem. **Get someone to do it anyway, early.** "It may seem expensive, but rather spend that money now than later, before you are pushed out."

His examples:

- A student founder abroad pays a small yearly fee for legal help so that his contracts and governance documents are done properly while the company is small.
- A tech client nearly lost an investment of about €8 million. When the investor's lawyers did **due diligence** (the checks an investor runs before putting in money), they asked for governance documents that did not exist. The team had to produce them in a rush, working day and night.

Every investor's lawyers will ask for these documents. Nobody starts a business expecting it to stay small, and growth needs outside money. So prepare the documents as if that investor is coming tomorrow.

Typical documents: founders' and shareholders' agreements, a company constitution or bylaws, board and decision rules, contracts with staff and clients, and IP ownership terms.

## Use it in the capstone

If the project might continue after the course, agree early who owns the code and IP and under what licence the public repo is released. Write it in the README or a team agreement.
