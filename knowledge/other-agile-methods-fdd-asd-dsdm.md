# Other Agile methods: FDD, ASD, DSDM

| | FDD | ASD | DSDM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full name, origin | Feature-Driven Development; De Luca and Coad, 1997 | Adaptive Software Development; Highsmith, 1990s (from RAD) | Dynamic Systems Development Method; UK consortium, 1994 (from RAD) |
| Central idea | Build small client-valued features | Adapt and learn amid uncertainty | **Fixed time and cost, flexible features** |
| Mechanism | Domain model → feature list → plan, design and build by feature | Speculate → Collaborate → Learn | MoSCoW + timeboxes; drop low-priority items, keep the deadline |
| Structure | 5 processes; chief architect, chief programmers, class owners | Light, philosophy-driven | Most formal: phases, business roles, 8 principles |
| Best for | Large teams, domains you can model | Complex, high-change work | Business projects with fixed deadlines |

FDD feature format: *action the result by/for/of/to an object*, for example "Calculate the total of an order". Each feature is two weeks or less.

What they share with Scrum and XP: the Agile Manifesto values. Incremental delivery, close customer involvement, working software, and welcoming change.

## Use it in the capstone

Our situation is DSDM's: the 18 Dec deadline and our time are fixed, so scope must flex. Use MoSCoW on the backlog, and state in the paper that we borrowed DSDM's fixed-time rule inside Scrum.
