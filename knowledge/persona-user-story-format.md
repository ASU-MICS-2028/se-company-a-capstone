# Persona user-story format

The lecturer asked teams to formalise each **persona** (a realistic profile of a key user) with an agile **user story** - a one-sentence statement of who wants what and why:

> As a *[user type, name, age]*, I want *[key features A, B, C...]* so that *[the outcome or experience they get]*.

His examples, paraphrased:
- As a visually impaired student, aged 24, I want my list highlighted and read out so that I can engage effectively with the cafeteria system.
- As a graduate student with ADHD, aged 26, I want features A to E so that I have a streamlined experience.

The story lists the key features you will provide for that person, so it links the persona straight to requirements. He also wants a **picture** with every persona so the audience can relate to the person.

In Lecture 4 he repeated this as an exam hint: personas need **detail** - a name (for example, a hearing-impaired student given a local name), the specific condition and needs - because that detail helps you relate to the person. The same story form appears in agile user stories ("As a ..., I want ... so that ...") with acceptance criteria.

The textbook offers other templates; read them for more perspectives, but this simple form is the one he wants used.

## Use it in the capstone

Write one user story per persona at the top of our UX doc, and make sure every feature in the story appears as a labelled requirement.
