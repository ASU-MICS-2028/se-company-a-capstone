# Gather evidence from real users

Design decisions must be backed by evidence, not stereotypes or what an AI tool finds online. The lecturer's practical recipe for a semester project:

- **Literature**: two or three research papers supporting your view of the users.
- **A short survey**: about three to five focused questions plus one open-ended question (even two plus one is fine if it gets enough information). Questions must be very clear.
- **Make it open**: hear from able users and from those who identify as disabled, to get different perspectives.
- **Anonymous and consented**: let people optionally disclose a condition (for example ADHD); don't force it.
- **Go through the right office**: on campus, the disability inclusion office and its accessibility service. They know who to contact, hold a database, and may set up the engagement for you. Talking to cafeteria staff and a student is a start, not enough.

He first "recommended", then "strongly advised", and finally said it is effectively a requirement. Users are also pleased to learn someone is looking into their experience.

## Use it in the capstone

Run a short survey and cite two or three papers in our UX doc and ACM paper. Record survey design and results as evidence for each persona and requirement.
