# Double Diamond

**Exam topic.** The lecturer pointed to the double diamond in the recommended UX textbook as the scientific structure for analysing users, and said plainly that it **will appear in the exam**. He expected teams to have applied it to their user analysis and was disappointed none had said so.

The process (popularised by the UK Design Council) has two diamonds. Each widens (diverge: explore many options) and then narrows (converge: choose). The first diamond is about the right problem; the second about the right solution.

1. **Discover (diverge).** Explore the problem space: observe and interview users, map journeys, run a design audit, look at qualitative data (interviews, surveys) and quantitative data (usage metrics), analyse competitors.
2. **Define (converge).** Synthesise the evidence into the core problem, user needs and personas. Prioritise with stakeholders and roughly size the effort ([T-shirt sizing](t-shirt-sizing.md)).
3. **Develop (diverge).** Generate alternative solutions. Sketch on paper first, then wireframe and prototype different interaction approaches.
4. **Deliver (converge).** Test, refine and evaluate the chosen solution through repeated usability tests and cross-team feedback before release, then gather feedback. Most of the time goes here.

What the grader wants: concrete activities and the evidence that drives each decision. The key danger is **solution fixation**, jumping to "build an app" before Discover. In the Bamboo Bike example, the real need was reliable, affordable, accessible short trips, and the answer might be kiosks, campus ID or SMS/USSD.

He paired this with a second hint: detailed, named **personas** with specific needs help you relate to the user.

## Use it in the capstone

Structure the UX doc's research section as the four phases, and say explicitly in the paper's Methodology that we used the double diamond, showing how the idea passed through Discover and Define *before* we chose the technology. Name one alternative solution we rejected and why.
