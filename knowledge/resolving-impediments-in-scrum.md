# Resolving impediments in Scrum

An **impediment** is anything that blocks progress: a technical problem, missing knowledge, or a disagreement about the solution. The lecturer said they are bound to happen in stand-ups and in planning.

His four steps:

1. **Identify it** out loud during the [daily scrum](daily-scrum.md) or sprint planning.
2. **Resolve it quickly, with facts.** If two people propose different solutions, give each about five minutes to explain theirs. The team decides on the evidence, not on seniority or emotion (see [empiricism](empiricism-in-scrum.md)).
3. **Assign an owner.** One named person does the research or brings in a helper, and reports back.
4. **Adapt the sprint plan.** Scrum is agile, so the plan can change whenever it needs to. Agree the change in the team, then explain it to the client so they stay confident.

He also warned against arrogance. Nobody is perfect. When something goes wrong, say "I didn't do well here; this is my plan to do better." Every iteration should include learning.

## Use it in the capstone

Add an "impediments" column to the stand-up log, with an owner and a resolution date. Recurring impediments are good input for the retrospective and for the paper's lessons-learned section.
