# Founder control trade-offs

**Founder control** means the person who started a company keeps decisive power, often through extra voting rights. In the Lecture 6 seminar a student argued that tough, aggressive founders built many famous companies. The groups weighed both sides.

**For founder control**
- It protects the founder's vision and lets them steer quickly.
- They can take risks without the delays of board bureaucracy.
- The founder may care most about the company's long-term interest.

**Against founder control**
- **Entrenchment**: the founder can set rules nobody else can change.
- Minority shareholders lose any real say.
- Accountability weakens (see [concentrated control weakens accountability](concentrated-control-weakens-accountability.md)).

**The middle way one group proposed:** give the founder strong control while the company is young. Then add checks and spread power as the company grows and takes outside money.

The session facilitator's view: of course a founder should have power, but **that power should be limited**. Choosing to limit your own power is a leadership decision. The founder who refuses may end up pushed out anyway. He described a founder he knew personally who was removed from a multi-million-dollar company he had built and never recovered.

## Use it in the capstone

Useful framing for team roles: a strong technical lead is fine, but write down where their authority ends and when the team or stakeholder decides instead.
