# Performance versus reliability

The lecturer drew a simple line between two quality attributes:

- **Performance** is about an **instance**. You sit on the chair and, in that second, it works as a chair. It performed.
- **Reliability** is about **performance over a period of time**. The chair is reliable when it keeps working, sitting after sitting.

His human example: you tell a friend a secret and they keep it - good. You can call them reliable only after they have kept your private information over a long time.

He also noted that availability is never 100%; you might aim for something like 99%. There is a formal way to work out these figures (taught later in the course).

## Use it in the capstone

In the quality engineering deliverable, measure performance with single-request tests (response time) and reliability over time (failures across a test period). Don't mix the two in the report.
