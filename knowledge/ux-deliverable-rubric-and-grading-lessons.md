# UX deliverable rubric and grading lessons

After the first team presentations (inclusive UX for a campus cafeteria), the lecturer explained his marking. The categories, roughly as read out (total about 100):

| Category | Max |
|---|---|
| Problem understanding and persona | 10 |
| User journey and UX analysis | 10 |
| Requirements and classification (with evidence, e.g. a classification table) | 20 |
| Requirements-to-design traceability | ~15 |
| Figma prototype | 20 |
| Accessibility and UX | 15 |
| Evaluation of the UX | 5 |
| Presentation | 5 |

What cost marks:
- **Shallow user analysis.** Not enough time spent understanding real users. This knocks on: incomplete requirements lead to incomplete analysis, which makes the evaluation defective.
- No evidence from real users (no survey, no contact with the disability office).
- Missing classification table on the slides (even if it was in the report).
- Small fonts and squeezed slides.

His standard: an **A means excellence, and excellence means thoroughness**. No team got an A on this round (both were around B+), and he said he would adjust after reading the documents. "Know your client or your user" is the message he wants students to remember.

## Use it in the capstone

Use this table as a self-check before submitting our requirements and UX docs: is every category evidenced, and is our user analysis thorough enough to earn an A?
