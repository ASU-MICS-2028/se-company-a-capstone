# Sprint retrospective

The **sprint retrospective** is a structured, team-only meeting after the [sprint review](sprint-review.md). The team inspects how it worked (people, tools, process, Definition of Done) and makes a plan to improve in the next sprint. For example: "the collaboration tool we used didn't work; what do we switch to?"

Techniques from the lecture and the course deck:

- **Start / Stop / Continue.** New practices to start, habits to stop, strengths to keep.
- **The four Ls.** What did we *like*, *learn*, *lack* and *long for*?
- **Mad / Sad / Glad.** Brings emotions and morale to the surface: clashes, frustration, a senior member who rubbed a newcomer the wrong way.

Planning and stand-ups run on "facts, not emotions", but people still have emotions. The retrospective is the place to deal with them openly so they don't fester. The aim is to resolve every issue, not fight.

The output matters more than the format. Turn insights into an **action plan**: each action gets an owner and a deadline and is tracked on the board, and the most important improvement goes into the next Sprint Backlog as a task. In one of the lecturer's projects, a retrospective found a design bottleneck, and the team added a designer for the next sprint.

## Use it in the capstone

The brief requires "sprint retrospective summaries". After each sprint, write a half-page in `docs/agile/retro-sprint-N.md`: technique used, top 3 points, actions with owners, and whether the last sprint's actions were done. These feed the paper's discussion and lessons-learned sections.
