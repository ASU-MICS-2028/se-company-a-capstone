# Safety incidents can end a product

The lecturer told the story of a past student start-up: a ride service on campus using tricycles, with a mobile app for booking. It ran well for about two years and was making money. Then one driver, who had already been warned about speeding twice, crashed on a sharp turn and injured students. Parents and families flooded the university with complaints. The university shut down all student transport services. The founder had to sell his vehicles, faced intense personal blame (in a small company, all the anger lands on the owner), and left the business.

The software worked. The failure was **operational risk**: risk from people and processes, not code. And the reaction was emotional, not technical.

Lessons the class drew, which the lecturer agreed with:

- **Launch with a clear disclaimer and written policies** (terms of use, a risk waiver) so responsibility is clear.
- **Train users** before letting them use risky physical products, such as a bike. Letting untrained people ride is irresponsible.
- **Use technology to reduce the risk where possible**, such as speed limits, monitoring and geofencing.
- **Protect the individual founder.** The university's lawyers, a statement and counselling helped, but the lecturer felt more could have been done.

A student pointed out that a food-ordering system has its own version of this (food poisoning). That is why the campus health centre also monitors vendors.

## Use it in the capstone

Add a risk and safety section to the requirements and the paper's ethics section. Include harms from the physical side of our system, not just software failures, and the policy or disclaimer we would ship with.
