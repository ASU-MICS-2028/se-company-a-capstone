# Accessibility and inclusive design

**Accessibility** means people with different abilities can use a system. **Inclusive** or **universal design** means designing from the start so as many people as possible are enabled.

The lecturer's points:
- **Disability is situational and universal.** Visiting a congregation where everyone signed, he could not follow; in that setting he was the one disabled. And everyone will be disabled at some point: strong people end up using walking sticks, sharp eyes need bifocals, hearing fades with age.
- So build accessibility in **first**, not after a complaint. Ask: how would my grandmother use this? Someone with dyslexia?
- Nobody expects a system that everyone can use, but you must make considerations for others. Even a small signpost to an accessible resource helps.
- Budget limits are real: you may analyse your population and cater for groups A and B first, but cater for more if you can.
- Most developers focus on functional requirements for able users and skip this.

He also urged learning to communicate with people in our own communities, such as learning sign language, since they may have ideas that solve problems.
