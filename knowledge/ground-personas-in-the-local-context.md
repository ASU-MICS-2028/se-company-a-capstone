# Ground personas in the local context

A **persona** is a made-up but realistic user you design for. In Lecture 7 the lecturer praised a team for replacing a generic "Jane Doe" persona with a locally named student who has a clear story: 15 minutes to get lunch before the next class, wants to avoid surprises about price and allergens, and gets distracted in the queue. Using the same persona all the way through the presentation made it easy to follow.

He also noted there were few **African illustrations** in the decks. Since the course is about African problems, the people, places and images should look like the users.

A copyright point came up too. One persona photo was AI-generated. The reviewers' first question was whether the team had rights to the image. Use images you made, licensed, or generated, and say which.

Checklist:

- A local name and setting that fit the actual users.
- A concrete time pressure or constraint, plus goals and frustrations.
- Use it consistently across the user story, diagrams and demo.
- Make sure you have the rights to any persona image.

## Use it in the capstone

Our personas should be clearly African in name, setting and images. Use them by name across the requirements, UX doc, diagrams and talk, and state where each image came from.
