# Software development lifecycle stages

The lecturer walked the class through the stages a software product goes through, from idea to long-term care. Books use different names, but the flow is:

1. **Planning** - research, analysis and ideation (generating and testing ideas). The aim is to check whether the problem is real and felt by other people, not just an idea you had in bed.
2. **Requirements specification** - write down, in detail, what the system must do. A requirement is "what is required of you"; the parts together form the solution.
3. **Design and analysis (with prototyping)** - represent the system in diagrams and build rough prototypes once you have a light idea of the product.
4. **Implementation** - build, always referring back to the requirements.
5. **Testing** - verification and validation, also called quality assurance (QA).
6. **Deployment** - release to an internal server, a production server, or a target device.
7. **Monitoring and maintenance** - watch the running system and fix or update it.

The methodology changes how you move through the stages: a structured (waterfall) approach goes through once with full requirements; an agile approach loops through design, prototype and build many times.

User experience (UX) is not a single stage. It sits mostly in requirements and design but affects every stage after.

## Use it in the capstone

Use this as the skeleton for the Scrum plan and for the "method" section of the ACM paper: say which stages each sprint covered.
