# Workplace culture is a governance issue

In the Uber case, a former engineer published a blog post saying HR had failed to act on her harassment complaints. Later, a board member made a sexist remark at the very meeting meant to address the company's culture. The session facilitator's point: **a culture problem is a governance matter, not just an HR problem.** It starts at the top. A CEO's behaviour sets the ethical tone for everyone below, and a toxic culture spreads downward.

What the groups said should happen:

- **The board should take an active role in culture.** Boards usually stay out of daily operations, but they should state the values they expect and check that they are lived.
- **Enforce policies.** The company probably had a harassment policy, but reports went nowhere. A policy that isn't enforced isn't a policy.
- **Have a clear escalation ladder.** If HR does nothing, there must be someone higher to report to, up to an independent board committee. That includes protected whistleblowing, meaning reporting wrongdoing without retaliation.
- **Accountability sits with leadership.** You can't fix a culture from the bottom.

The facilitator also noted that some companies with terrible cultures are still profitable, because investors often care most about returns. That is why culture needs governance rules and cannot be left to market pressure.

## Use it in the capstone

Agree a team code of conduct and an escalation path (team, then Scrum Master, then lecturer) at the start. The paper's ethics section can mention how team conduct was handled.
