# Personas from evidence

A **persona** is a short portrait of a realistic user: goals, context, frustrations, abilities and technology access. It stands in for a group of real users during design.

What the course rewards:
- **Evidence-informed, not stereotyped.** Build each persona from research findings. "Students with ADHD are disorganised" is a stereotype. "Loses place when interrupted mid-checkout" is a barrier observed in research.
- Include **accessibility and technology factors**: impairments, data access, device, digital literacy.
- Pair each persona with **scenarios** showing them using the system in context.
- Explain **how personas reduce assumptions**: they force the team to design for a named person, not an imaginary "average user". Designing for one typical user excludes everyone else.

Interesting fact: in Ghimire and Charters' survey, personas were the *least* used Agile practice (18% of respondents).

## Use it in the capstone

Write 2–3 personas for the proposal from our interviews, including at least one with an access constraint (low bandwidth, low literacy or an impairment). Cite each persona's evidence in a footnote.
