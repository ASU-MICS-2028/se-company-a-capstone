# Ghimire and Charters 2022: Agile practices and project outcomes

**Question.** What impact do the Agile practices a team uses, internally and with the customer, have on project outcomes?

**Method.** The authors built a list of 20 common practices from the literature, checked by five practitioners with 15+ years' experience. They ran an online Qualtrics questionnaire (about 18 minutes) across New Zealand over 5 months: **73 responses from 50 organisations**, 57% developers. Respondents ticked the practices they used and reported team and customer challenges, timeliness and budget. The analysis used Pearson correlation in SPSS.

**Findings.**
- Every respondent used at least one practice. The mean was **11 practices**. Only 2 used all 20.
- Most used: **stand-ups (90%)**, then backlog, unit tests and user stories (57 each), Scrumban, and retrospectives. Least used: **personas (18%)**.
- More practices correlated with **fewer challenges** in team communication (r = −0.272), in defining requirements with the customer, and in setting priorities. All three were significant.
- Not significant: sharing ideas (r = −0.206), timeline (r = −0.215), budget (r = 0.082).
- 53% reported finishing on time. 40% did not know their budget.

**Conclusion.** "Agile-lite", adopting only a few practices, does not give good outcomes. Fuller adoption goes with better communication, requirements and priorities.

**Limits** (the course deck stresses these): self-reported data, one country, modest sample, and **correlation, not causation**.

## Use it in the capstone

Cite it to justify using a *bundle* of practices (stand-ups, backlog, user stories, retros, CI, unit tests, burndown) rather than Scrum in name only. Mirror its honest limitations section in our own paper.
