# Daily scrum

The **daily scrum** (or daily stand-up) is a short, focused meeting, usually 15 minutes, held every day of the sprint. The developers check progress toward the [sprint goal](sprint-goal.md) and change the sprint backlog if needed.

**Structure.** Each person answers three questions in turn, then passes to the next person:

1. What did I do since the last meeting? ("I finished secure payments and will commit it to the repo.")
2. What will I do next?
3. What is blocking me? ("I don't understand how ADHD affects menu design.")

If someone is blocked, solve it fast: someone who has done it before shares code or offers "ten minutes of your time today." Otherwise, point the person to the right resource and keep the meeting moving.

**Rules from the lecturer's practice:**

- **It's a commitment.** Pick a time the team can always keep. No "I'm sleepy today."
- **Change the format to fit the project.** On a complex project his team used 30 minutes. Later in the project, when most work was done, they met every other day.
- **Don't bring the same blocker twice.** The first time, you get help. If you come back with the same unsolved problem, you may get a "strike", because others may depend on you. After repeated misses (he said about three), the task goes to someone else or to a new sub-team.

## Use it in the capstone

Keep a short log of each stand-up (date, the three answers per person, blockers). It is the easiest Scrum record to produce and the hardest to fake later.

## Also in Lecture 5

- The lecturer recommended a timebox of no more than one hour; the Scrum Master keeps each person to about two minutes and shortens the box if the team regularly finishes early (see [Timeboxing](timeboxing.md)).
- Others chime in with help ("I've done NFC payments, I'll join you", "check this GitHub repo"), then the next person speaks.
- A team that can't meet daily can fix one regular slot (his team meets weekly). When stakeholders join that slot, it doubles as the sprint review; a retrospective can use it too, with a longer box.
