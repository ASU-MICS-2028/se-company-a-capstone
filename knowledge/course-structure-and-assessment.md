# Course structure and assessment

What the lecturer said about how the course runs:

- **One project, end to end, over five to six weeks**: ideation and planning, user experience, then a thorough requirements pass, budgeting, architecture, QA/testing, and ethics. About two weeks each on the key parts: UX, architectural design, and QA. He was most excited to see the architecture.
- If the requirements document is comprehensive enough, the dedicated requirements week may be skipped and building starts.
- **No coding is taught in class.** Data structures will be discussed, but students build on their own.
- **Journaling and deliverables** are submitted every two weeks through a Canvas slot.
- **Quiz**: online on Canvas, scenario-based: a scenario with about three to five questions where you relate theory to it; no multiple choice. Leaving the quiz page is flagged and reported. Use reliable internet.
- **Read the recommended UX textbook**; the class doesn't lecture from slides, the book carries the theory. He pointed to the **double diamond** method (discover, define, develop, deliver) as the structure for user research.
- Each team must pick a **company name**.
- See [Quiz and exam expectations](quiz-and-exam-expectations.md) for later hints and submission formats.

## Use it in the capstone

Plan our sprints around these phases and keep a journal entry per sprint for the two-weekly submission.
