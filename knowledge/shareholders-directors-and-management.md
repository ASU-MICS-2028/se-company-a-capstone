# Shareholders, directors and management

The session facilitator laid out the basic structure of a company:

- **Shareholders** own the company. They put in the money.
- **Directors** (the board) are appointed by the shareholders to oversee and run the company.
- **Management** is hired by the directors. Management then hires everyone else; directors don't hire the cleaner.

Reports flow up the chain: staff report to management, management reports to the directors, and the directors report the state of the company to the shareholders at shareholders' meetings.

**Reserved matters.** Some decisions are too big for directors to take alone and need shareholder approval:

- changing the company's line of business (for example, from making tobacco to making tumblers)
- selling or buying a large share of the company's assets
- mergers and acquisitions

Why: directors can be overzealous, for example chasing profit too hard. The rules exist so they cannot overdo it. Each group has things it can do alone, things it cannot do, and things it can do only with another group's approval. That is good governance.

## Use it in the capstone

Map the same idea onto the team. The client or stakeholder approves scope (like shareholders). The product owner and Scrum Master steer (like directors). Developers deliver (like management and staff). Write down which decisions need whose approval.
