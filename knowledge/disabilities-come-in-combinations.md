# Disabilities come in combinations

"We always think that disability comes as one," the lecturer said, and that is a mistake. A hearing-impaired person may also be colour-blind; a visually impaired person may also be hearing impaired. He cited a law graduate from a leading US university who is both deaf and blind.

Implications:
- Don't design interventions as separate packages ("visual impairment: take this; hearing impairment: take that"). A system built for one group in isolation can fail people with more than one need.
- Think of a **matrix**: user needs on one axis, interventions on the other, and check coverage.
- Layer each new group onto earlier work. The class added hearing impairment on top of their ADHD and visual-impairment designs.
- Many interventions help everyone, including foreigners and able users (clear categories, prices, pictures).

## Use it in the capstone

Build a small needs-by-intervention matrix in the UX doc to show coverage across our user groups.
