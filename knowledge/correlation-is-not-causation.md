# Correlation is not causation

A **correlation** says two measures move together. A **Pearson r** runs from −1 to +1: positive means they rise together, negative means one falls as the other rises, and near 0 means no linear link. It does not prove that one *causes* the other.

Ghimire and Charters found that teams using more Agile practices reported fewer communication problems (r = −0.272). Possible explanations other than "practices cause better communication":
- **Reverse cause:** teams that already communicate well find it easier to adopt practices.
- **Confounders:** a third factor, such as experienced staff or a supportive company, drives both.
- **Measurement bias:** self-reported survey answers.

**Statistical significance** says a result is unlikely to be pure chance. It does not say the effect is large or causal. An r of −0.27 is a weak to moderate effect.

## Use it in the capstone

In the paper's threats-to-validity section, say what our evaluation can and cannot claim. For example: "Five users rated the app easier than the paper menu; with no control group and a small sample, this suggests but does not prove improved usability."
