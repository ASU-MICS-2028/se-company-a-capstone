# Scrum lecture deck summary

**Argument.** Plan-driven development (fixed requirements, late feedback, big upfront design) suits stable problems. Agile suits changing requirements through customer collaboration, incremental delivery and fast feedback. Scrum is the main Agile framework.

**Structure of the deck:**
1. **Scrum at a glance:** product goal → product backlog → sprint planning → sprint → increment → review and feedback → backlog adaptation.
2. **Empiricism:** transparency, inspection, adaptation.
3. **Scrum team:** Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers. No hierarchy, shared accountability.
4. **The sprint as an engineering loop:** planning → daily scrum → build, test, integrate → review → retrospective → next sprint, around a Sprint Goal and a Definition of Done.
5. **Artefacts and commitments:** product backlog ↔ product goal, sprint backlog ↔ sprint goal, increment ↔ Definition of Done.
6. **Practices that matter,** backed by Ghimire and Charters' survey (stand-ups at 90%). More practices correlate with better outcomes, though correlation is not causation.
7. **African case: "CampusRide Ghana"**, a campus mobility app. Its 2-week Sprint Goal is to deliver the core booking flow with tracking and payments. Stakeholders: students, university administration, transport operators, municipal officials.

**Takeaways:** value, feedback, transparency, team collaboration.

## Use it in the capstone

The CampusRide board is a model for ours: user stories in "As a … I can …" form across To Do / In Progress / Done, with a Sprint Goal at the top and stakeholders listed.
