# Becoming a Scrum Master takes practice

The **Scrum Master** makes sure the team follows Scrum well. They guide fair estimation, set up the work so people can work in parallel, remove blockers, and answer for planning failures. The lecturer stressed that this role rests on **experience, not a certificate**.

His points:

- He has worked in Scrum teams for about seven years and still doesn't call himself a Scrum Master. He works under a mentor with more than thirty years of experience. That mentor has led large projects end to end, including an IT and website reimagining for a national institution.
- **The path is mentorship.** Newer practitioners are mentored over a long period until an experienced Scrum Master judges they understand the principles. Then they sit certifications. There are three or four practitioner-level certifications before the Scrum Master level.
- **Certificates without practice mean little.** People who memorise the theory can pass, but employers ask how long you have *practised*. Some excellent practitioners have no certificate.
- **When you're new, observe first.** He compared it to a new member of parliament who listens for a while before speaking.
- He hoped students would earn at least one certification during the follow-on agile methods course.

He set a research task: **how does a Scrum Master differ from a project manager?** The roles overlap but are not the same. Project-management certifications include PRINCE2 and those based on the PMBOK guide.

## Use it in the capstone

Rotate or clearly assign the Scrum Master role in our team, and note in the Scrum records who held it each sprint. Be ready to compare Scrum Master and project manager in the interview.
