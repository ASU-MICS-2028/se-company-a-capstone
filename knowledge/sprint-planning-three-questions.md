# Sprint planning answers three questions

**Sprint planning** is the meeting that starts every sprint. Following the Scrum Guide (scrumguides.org, 2020 edition), it answers three questions:

1. **Why does this sprint matter?** The client's reason, for example "we need this feature before a competitor ships theirs." That reason becomes the [sprint goal](sprint-goal.md).
2. **What can be delivered?** The exact product: is the accessible menu a website, a mobile app, an SMS service, or a mix? The lecturer pointed out that teams often forget to say this. Knowing the product type drives everything else.
3. **How will the work get done?** Who does what (developers, QA, testers), how long it will take, and what tools, devices or environments it needs.

How the lecturer runs it in practice:

- **Research inside the meeting.** Give everyone 5–10 minutes to look up how others solve the problem (IEEE, ACM and journal papers, existing products). Each person reports back in about 30 seconds. List the features, settle conflicts, and agree.
- **Use the team's experience to size the work.** For example: "Last time a screen reader feature took us four days. The new AI part adds half a day, and we keep half a day for testing."
- **The product owner takes cost and scope back to the client.** The product owner is the person who speaks for the client and orders the backlog. If the budget is too small, the client drops items. When both sides agree, the sprint starts.

## Use it in the capstone

Keep minutes for each sprint planning meeting with the three answers written down. They are cheap, strong evidence for the Scrum records.
