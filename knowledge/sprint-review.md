# Sprint review

The **sprint review** happens at the end of each sprint. The Scrum team shows the **increment** (the working result of the sprint) to the stakeholders and gathers their feedback. Then it updates the product backlog.

**Who comes:** all relevant stakeholders, not just the client contact. For the class's cafeteria example: the cafeteria management and some workers, the campus facilities team that manages them, and health and safety.

**Rules:**

- **Demo working features only.** Don't say "we had this feature but it doesn't work"; stakeholders don't care. Show how it works, live, on a staging server (see [deploy demos to a staging server](deploy-demos-to-a-staging-server.md)).
- **Turn feedback into backlog items.** A stakeholder will often ask "have you considered…?" Add it as a new user story for a later sprint. This is the "adapt" step. The lecturer said it is unusual to finish a review with nothing to add.
- **Get approval.** Approval at the review is what lets the work move to production.

The review is between the team and the stakeholders. The [sprint retrospective](sprint-retrospective.md) that follows is for the team only.

## Use it in the capstone

Run at least one review with a real stakeholder from our problem domain. Record who attended, what we showed, and which new backlog items came out of it. That makes strong evidence for the Scrum records and the paper's evaluation section.
