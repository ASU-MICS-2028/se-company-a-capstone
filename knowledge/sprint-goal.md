# Sprint goal

A **sprint goal** is one objective for one sprint. It gives the team focus but leaves room to change how the work is done. Every product has a **product goal** (the long-term aim, shaped by the product vision). Each sprint goal is a smaller step derived from it, so the team stays on track.

What a good sprint goal looks like:

- **A single sentence**, not a list. It wraps up all the stories chosen for the sprint.
- **Specific and focused**, straight to the point.
- **Measurable where possible**, so the client can check it at the [sprint review](sprint-review.md).

The lecturer's example: *"By the end of this sprint, students can browse the cafeteria menu and place a meal order digitally, reducing queue wait time by 50%."* The number matters. Measure today's wait time first. If you finish and you have not reached 50%, the client will challenge you at the review.

The product owner shapes the goal's content. The **Scrum Master** (the person who coaches the team on Scrum practice) makes sure it does not get too complex.

## Use it in the capstone

Write a one-line, measurable sprint goal at the top of each sprint record. It also gives the conference talk an easy story: goal, increment, result.

## Also in Lecture 5

The sprint goal is often the backlog item chosen in sprint planning: pick item one from the product backlog, and it becomes the sprint's goal. Keep sprints focused on about two items (three for a bigger team). The product goal itself should be short with the key words in it, for example "an inclusive cafeteria", naming the value added.
