# Agile versus waterfall

The lecturer named two main families of development methodology:

- **Structured** methods, with **waterfall** as the main example. Waterfall expects a thorough, complete definition of all requirements before building. You move through the stages in order and do not go back. It suits some projects (for example, where the full plan must be fixed, like a building) and not others.
- **Iterative** or **agile** methods. You do not need a complete picture. Take the two or three requirements you understand, dig deep into them, build, and then expand. Design and prototyping repeat in loops before moving on.

Consequences he drew out:
- In waterfall, user analysis has to be exhaustive up front: you must understand every potential user and plan for them.
- In agile, after each implementation you may iterate again or proceed, depending on what you learned.
- The choice also depends on the kind of product and its feasibility.

## Use it in the capstone

We are using Scrum (an agile method). State this in the Scrum records and paper, and explain why: a six-week build with uncertain requirements suits iteration better than waterfall.

## Also in ICS 530 Scrum lecture deck; personal notes: Agile Methodologies/Notes

The Scrum deck frames the contrast as **plan-driven vs adaptive**:

| Plan-driven | Adaptive (Agile) |
|---|---|
| Fixed requirements, hard to change | Requirements evolve as we learn |
| Customer involved only at milestones | Continuous customer collaboration |
| Working software and feedback come late | Small increments delivered often |
| Big design up front | Rapid feedback loops |

Scrum rests on **empiricism**: knowledge comes from experience and observation, so decide on what you can see. A third option from the concurrency-teaching-tool dissertation is **evolutionary prototyping** (see [Evolutionary vs throwaway prototyping](evolutionary-vs-throwaway-prototyping.md)).
