# Design for languages that are spoken more than written

Many Ghanaian languages are spoken far more than they are read. A text interface in Twi still excludes people who speak Twi but can't read it. The lecturer's advice: augment text with speech (text-to-speech, automated voice calls) and, where possible, sign language. Ghana has a formal sign language that overlaps heavily with American Sign Language, plus several community sign languages.

He also described his Kalangu project, which picked languages for reach across borders: Hausa, Swahili and Arabic, plus French and some Portuguese. And he asked how a sick person who is blind or cannot speak would interact with a hospital system at all.

## Use it in the capstone

Twɛn's USSD and SMS are text. A natural extension, and a strong answer in Q&A, is an automated voice call that reads the "come now" message in Twi. Desk tickets and staff help remain the route for patients who can use neither.
