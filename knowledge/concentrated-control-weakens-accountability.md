# Concentrated control weakens accountability

**Accountability** means a person must justify their actions to someone who can act on the answer. The session facilitator's lesson from the Uber case: **when control is concentrated in one person, accountability gets weaker.**

How it happened in the case:

- The founder held **super-voting rights**: shares that carry more votes than ordinary shares, giving him a majority of the board vote.
- He handpicked the senior team, so the people he was supposed to answer to became his allies.
- Challenging him cost minority shareholders more than staying quiet, so nobody did.
- After resigning as CEO he **kept his board seat and voting power**. Groups said this left the next CEO with divided staff loyalty, open resistance to reforms that implied the old era was wrong, and a situation where the new CEO had to manage the founder instead of the company.

The remedy groups proposed is **separation of powers**: spread decision rights across independent people and bodies so that no single vote decides everything.

The same idea applies to software teams. If one person holds the only admin keys, makes all the merges and owns all the knowledge, nobody can challenge their mistakes, and the team stalls when they are away.

## Use it in the capstone

Spread repository admin rights, deployment access and decision-making across at least two people. Require pull-request reviews so no single member can merge unchecked.
