# Burnup chart

A **burnup chart** shows **work completed** rising over time, plotted against a separate **total scope** line. Both are in story points, over the sprint days.

Its advantage over a burndown is that it separates two causes of missing a target:
- the *completed* line rises too slowly (delivery problem), or
- the *scope* line rises (**scope creep**: work added after planning).

Example from my notes: a team planned 40 points, and 10 more were added mid-sprint. It completed 43 points, more than planned, yet finished 7 short of the new 50. A burndown would make the team look slow. The burnup shows the real cause was scope creep.

## Use it in the capstone

Use a burnup for the whole project (release level), with scope as total backlog points. It honestly shows instructor-driven scope changes in the retro and the paper's discussion.
