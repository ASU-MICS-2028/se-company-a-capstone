# Capstone project brief

The final project **simulates industry-level software engineering**. Each company must conceptualise, design, implement, test, document and present an **intelligent software system that solves a real-world problem**, in about 6 weeks. It is a **new project, separate from the earlier concept project** (Lecture 10).

**Selection criteria.** The idea must meet all five (brief sections 1–2, repeated in Lecture 13):

1. **Real-world challenge or opportunity**, preferably African or Ghanaian, or tied to national priorities: health, sustainability, education, governance, mobility, climate, culture, employment, digital finance, agriculture.
2. **Intelligent computing**, with at least one of: ML or applied AI; data-driven analytics; intelligent interfaces or adaptive UX; algorithmic decision support; autonomy or smart sensing.
3. **End-to-end software engineering**: requirements elicitation, architecture, UX, implementation, testing, deployment planning and maintenance. It must be "more than a prototype".
4. **Feasible in 6 weeks**: ambitious but achievable. "Scope discipline is part of the assessment." The lecturer will help cut scope.
5. **Supports rigorous analysis**: every idea justified, architectural trade-offs and patterns, availability and reliability calculations, and enough substance for an ACM-style paper.

The project must integrate all six course themes: SE overview, UX, Agile (Scrum), architectures and programming models, quality engineering (availability, reliability, dependability), and applied data programming (pipelines, modelling, analytics, APIs).

**How to choose:** brainstorm individually (AI is fine for this), justify each idea's *impact*, pitch to the team, vote, and agree on one. AI use is allowed "as much as you want", but it needs solid prompt engineering, and the work must rest on research and real user needs.

Note: the brief says "groups of two"; our Company has six. Expect the scope to be judged against team size.

## Use it in the capstone

Score every candidate idea 0–2 on each criterion before the half-page idea submission, and keep the voting record in the Scrum repo. Make the intelligent component small but genuinely working: the rubric scores "intelligent/data-driven components" separately.
