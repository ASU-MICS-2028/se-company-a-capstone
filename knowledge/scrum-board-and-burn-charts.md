# Scrum board and burn charts

A **Scrum board** makes work visible. The lecturer showed his own (Trello for his team; a **GitHub project board** for classes, because it ties into continuous integration, and an AI plugin can help set it up quickly).

Typical columns: **product backlog**, **sprint backlog**, **in progress**, **sprint review** (ready for stakeholders), **complete**, plus **documentation** and updates.

Each card can hold:
- a user story ("As a course provider, I want a roadmap of milestones so that...")
- **acceptance criteria** and a checklist; ticking items gives a percentage, so progress is measurable
- an assignee and a due date ("you have two days")
- comments, links to people or repositories who can help

**Burn charts** connect analytics to the board:
- **Burn-down**: work completed and ticked off over time.
- **Burn-up**: work added, for instance when one backlog item turns out to be three big items.

Together they make progress visible and risks clear. Other agile practices he listed: daily stand-ups, user stories, acceptance criteria, backlog refinement, sprint planning (matching work to value and capacity), retrospectives, CI, and testing/QA.

**Be truthful** on the board and in stand-ups. Saying "almost done" when you aren't wrecks the timeline; honesty lets the team reassign help.

## Use it in the capstone

Run our sprints on the repo's GitHub project board and export a burn-down chart per sprint as evidence in the Scrum records.
