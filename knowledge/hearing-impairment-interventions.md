# Hearing impairment interventions

Walking a Deaf person through the cafeteria journey, the class and lecturer listed interventions:

**At the menu**
- Screens or clear printed menus (they can read), good signage showing where to go next.
- **Categorise** food (rice dishes, soups, stews, add-ons) and show **prices**, or at least price bands such as full or half meal.
- Check that colour choices are perceivable by everyone.
- A **QR code** linking to an app with pictures, ingredients, dietary information, and a chatbot or call option to ask questions.

**At payment**
- A poster or screen showing payment steps; "scan to pay" emphasised.

**At collection**
- Order numbers on a display, **push notifications** and **vibration** on the phone.
- A speech-to-text / text-to-speech app so staff can talk and the customer reads, and vice versa; sign-to-text is being researched.
- A **sign-language avatar** or captions on screens.
- **Human help**: a greeter, a priority queue, a signing staff member, lowered counter sections for wheelchair users.

He warned against using "a sledgehammer to kill a fly": sign language is good, but simple screens and notifications may do. And **equity is not the same as equality** - include people in what everyone does, while making provisions for them.

## Use it in the capstone

Use this as a checklist when we list accessibility interventions, then cost them in the budget trade-off.
