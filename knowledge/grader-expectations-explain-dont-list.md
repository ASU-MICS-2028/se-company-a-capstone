# Grader expectations: explain, don't list

The instructor's marking schemes repeat the same message: **marks come from reasoning and evidence, not from naming things.**

From the Quiz 1 (Bamboo Bike) scheme:
- "A strong answer should avoid simply listing methods without explaining their purpose."
- Double Diamond: no full marks for writing "Discover → Define → Develop → Deliver". You must say what you would *do* and *what evidence would change decisions*.
- Challenge the framing. The best answers reject "students need an app" and restate the real need: reliable, affordable, accessible short journeys. The answer might then be an app, physical controls, campus ID, kiosks, SMS or USSD.
- Personas must be "evidence-informed representations", not invented stereotypes.
- Stakeholders are not all direct users.
- Scrum is not "a sequence of meetings". Explain feedback and adaptation.
- Mid-sprint problems: never just "add the new requirement to the Sprint". Protect the Sprint Goal and use the findings to reorder the backlog.

From the mid-semester interview rubric: write requirements as "The system shall…" statements, then trace how each one enters the Product Backlog, gets acceptance criteria, is prioritised and refined, and becomes part of an Increment.

## Use it in the capstone

Apply this to the paper, the documentation and Q&A. Every artefact (persona, pattern, architecture choice) needs a one-line "because" backed by evidence, plus the alternatives we rejected.
