# Acceptance criteria must be specific and simple

**Acceptance criteria** are the checks that tell you a user story is finished. A **user story** is a short need written from the user's side ("As a student, I want to browse today's menu by category so that I can order quickly").

The lecturer's rules for writing them:

- **Make each one testable with a number where you can.** "The menu loads fast" cannot be checked. "The menu board loads in under 15 milliseconds" can: you run the test, and it either passes (tick it off) or fails (go back and fix it).
- **Use plain words.** Pick "simple and clear" over a fancy term. Users and testers may come from many countries, so avoid local slang or brand names that some people will not recognise.
- **Pair words with clear images** on user-facing screens, for example a food name next to a plain photo with no busy background or shiny reflections.
- **Keep sentences short.** Each criterion should be something you can tick off ("first one, check; second one, check"). Long sentences make criteria ambiguous.
- **Point back to value.** Every criterion should trace to what the client is paying for. The product owner keeps this link clear.

Criteria for a story add up to that story's "done". Criteria across all the stories in a sprint add up to the [Definition of Done](definition-of-done.md).

## Use it in the capstone

Every user story in our requirements doc and Scrum backlog needs measurable acceptance criteria. Those same numbers (load time, error rate) become test cases in the test plan.
