# Design for in-person and digital tracks

The lecturer's main critique of the first UX projects: teams thought "we are IT students, so let's build an app" and missed the **whole experience**.

Every user can engage in at least two ways, and you should model both:
- **In person** - they choose to walk in. You can't stop them, so streamline that visit (signage, kiosks or self-order screens like fast-food chains use, order numbers called on a display).
- **Virtual** - they order from their phone before or after class and collect later, or have it delivered.

Beyond software, make **policy recommendations** to the client. Example: if disabled students find the cafeteria too crowded, recommend discounted delivery paid by the university, or a pickup table with labelled orders. A slide near the end can say: "We built X, but a better solution would also need Y."

Asked whether this goes out of scope, he said yes, capture it as policy recommendations rather than building it.

## Use it in the capstone

Our design should show both the digital flow and the offline or in-person flow of our African users, and the paper's discussion section can list policy recommendations for the client.
