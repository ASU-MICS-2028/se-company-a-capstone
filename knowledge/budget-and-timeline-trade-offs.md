# Budget and timeline trade-offs

Early UX work imagines the best experience (augmented reality, avatars, kiosks). Then reality bites: what does each cost and how long does it take?

The lecturer's exercise:
1. List the technologies or interventions for your focal user groups.
2. Work within a fixed budget (about **US$10,000**, covering both technology and **labour**) and a strict timeline shown on a **Gantt chart** (a bar chart of tasks over time).
3. Make **trade-offs**: "this is effective, but that one does it better, so drop this"; "this one is essential, so keep it even though it's expensive". Justify every choice.

On order: requirements come before budgeting, because you need the full list to cost it. You can draft an initial budget and update it once requirements are complete. A student asked whether to design first and cut later; his answer was that the budget and timeline constraints come next, after the experience is imagined.

## Use it in the capstone

Include a costed feature list and a Gantt chart in our planning or Scrum records, and record which features were cut and why.
