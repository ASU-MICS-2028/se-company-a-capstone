# Hearing impairment and Deaf identity

Before designing for a group, understand who is in it. The lecturer walked the class through hearing impairment:

- A **hearing-impaired** person has partial or total inability to hear or understand sound. Definitions from health bodies use a hearing threshold: roughly more than 20-25 decibels (dB) of loss counts as impairment, rising to total deafness. It can affect one ear or both.
- **Hearing aids** amplify sound for people with some hearing.
- People with hearing impairment can usually **see, read and write**; the barrier is audio. Someone deaf from childhood may also not speak.
- **deaf** (small d) refers to the medical condition. **Deaf** (capital D) refers to a cultural identity: belonging to the Deaf community. Say "the Deaf community" with a capital D.
- At the university, support must be backed by medical evidence as well as self-identification.

He chose to design for total hearing loss because it is clearer to reason about than varying partial loss.

## Use it in the capstone

If our users include people with hearing loss, define the group precisely in the UX doc and use correct terminology in the paper.
