# No software is bug-free

The lecturer was blunt: there is no software system in the world that is 100% bug-free, and nobody should claim otherwise.

What that means in practice:
- Fix the serious bugs, the ones that affect service.
- Some small glitches are not worth the money to fix. You decide whether to spend resources on them or let them be.
- Companies often release once the product is **decently functional with no major issues**, then keep pushing updates and patches while it is in use. Competition forces this: if you wait to perfect everything, someone else ships first.
- But testing still has to be thorough. A public failure can be very costly. His example: a major tech company's early AI chatbot made bad errors in its launch demo, had to be pulled and rebranded, while competitors raced ahead.

So: test thoroughly, not perfectly; ship when it is functional; patch as you go.

## Use it in the capstone

In the test plan and paper, list known open defects and explain why each was accepted. This is honest and matches what the lecturer expects.
