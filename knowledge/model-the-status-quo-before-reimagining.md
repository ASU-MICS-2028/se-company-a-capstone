# Model the status quo before reimagining

In class, the lecturer and students drew how the campus cafeteria works **today**, before proposing improvements. The order matters:

1. Understand the users and the current system as it is (the **status quo**).
2. Identify the problems.
3. Only then reimagine and fix.

While drawing, the class kept disagreeing: is the meal plan chosen during ordering or payment? Who hands food to the delivery rider - kitchen or ordering staff? The lecturer's point: this confusion exists because the teams **did not engage users and staff thoroughly enough**. The gaps get filled with assumptions, biases and perceptions. Like the blind men and the elephant, they had seen three sides out of six. The fix is to go back, observe and ask.

He also warned against a common habit: "I did this on another project" or "I learned this technology in class, let me give it to them". You must solve **their** problem. A good software engineer listens carefully and then provides solutions; don't push "every restaurant does this". Studying best practice elsewhere (other campus food outlets) is still useful.

## Use it in the capstone

Before designing our solution, document the current process for our African problem (an as-is diagram) using real stakeholder input, and mark assumptions clearly.
