# Prototyping to understand the problem

A **prototype** is a quick, rough version of a product built to learn, not to ship. The lecturer moved prototyping out of the planning stage and into design: you start prototyping once you have a light idea of what the product should do.

His example: tie a black plastic bag to a drone and fly it. Water travels fine. Put something hot inside and the bag bends, and the load falls. That failure tells you that you did not understand the problem well enough, so you think again. A prototype helps you both understand the problem and find a good solution.

Order matters:
- First, confirm the problem exists and is felt by others (talk to friends, family, potential users).
- Then formulate a light solution ("a container on a drone that flies to me, I pay, it flies back").
- Then add detail (what kind of container: metal, plastic, paper?). That is where requirements begin.

Do not jump from an idea in your head straight to production.

## Use it in the capstone

Our prototype deliverable should be used to test our understanding of the African problem we chose. Record what each prototype taught us in the UX doc and in sprint reviews.
