# Negotiating scope and budget with the client

Scrum work starts with a client conversation. The lecturer described these steps:

1. **The client brings a problem.** Example: "our cafeteria services must be accessible to all students." The team turns it into a **product goal** and a product vision, then into backlog items.
2. **Agree on sprint length.** The client knows its market. If competitors ship updates weekly, a two-week sprint may be too slow. Offer something like "three items in two weeks", and change it if the client needs faster.
3. **Agree on scope.** Covering every kind of accessibility at once is rarely affordable, so the product owner asks the client to pick one or two to focus on.
4. **Agree on cost.** During planning the team lists the devices, facilities and environments it needs and what they cost. The product owner explains this to the client. If the money is short, the client removes items. Only after agreement (and payment) does the sprint start.
5. **Make it a contract.** Early in your career, have someone with legal knowledge involved. Experienced product owners build up their own contract templates.

The client pays for value. Keep checking that each item really serves what they contracted you for.

## Use it in the capstone

Treat our problem owner or stakeholder as "the client". Write down the agreed scope (including what we will *not* do) and the resource budget. The lecturer also asked teams to prepare a budget in case an idea gets built.
